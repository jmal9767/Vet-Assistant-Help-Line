# California scope guide for the Vet Assistant Help Line

**Operational guidance only; not legal advice.** This review is based on California sources current on September 20, 2026. Rules may differ when the operator or animal is outside California. Before accepting paying clients, obtain advice for the actual business model and jurisdictions served.

## What California law establishes

California Business and Professions Code section 4825 prohibits practicing veterinary medicine without a current license. Section 4826 includes diagnosing and prescribing a drug, appliance, application, or treatment for an animal within the practice of veterinary medicine. Section 4826 also restricts how treatments may be administered by veterinary assistants.

California defines veterinary telehealth as using electronic communications to deliver veterinary medicine, including diagnosis, consultation, care management, or treatment. Section 4826.6 states that only a California-licensed veterinarian may practice veterinary medicine by telehealth on an animal patient located in California.

The California Veterinary Medical Board explains that veterinarians alone diagnose medical problems and prescribe therapies. A disclaimer does not make an otherwise clinical diagnosis, prescription, or treatment lawful.

## Required boundary for this service

This help line must remain a general educational service. It must not:

- Identify or suggest a diagnosis for a specific animal.
- Recommend or prescribe medication, supplements, doses, applications, or treatments.
- Give individualized treatment, wound-care, or home-remedy instructions.
- Interpret laboratory results, imaging, medical records, or medication lists to reach a clinical conclusion.
- Predict an outcome or state that veterinary care is unnecessary.
- Represent that a veterinarian reviewed the question or that a veterinarian-client-patient relationship exists.

Charging for a service, providing it free, adding a disclaimer, or communicating by email, text, or phone does not change these boundaries.

## Appropriate content

- General healthy-pet husbandry, grooming, enrichment, and routine-care education.
- Public information about what commonly happens at veterinary visits.
- Helping an owner organize observations and questions to discuss with a veterinarian.
- Directing an owner to licensed veterinarians, emergency hospitals, poison control, or other public resources.
- Listing general emergency warning signs and directing the owner to immediate veterinary care without deciding that a specific animal is safe to wait.

## Emergency handling

For possible breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure, poisoning, major trauma, uncontrolled bleeding, inability to urinate, severe pain, pale or blue gums, heatstroke, eye injury, difficult labor, or another urgent concern, tell the person to contact an emergency veterinarian immediately. Do not provide case-specific first aid or ask them to wait for a help-line response.

ASPCA Animal Poison Control: (888) 426-4435. A consultation fee may apply.

## Before every response

1. Remove any diagnosis, dose, prescription, treatment plan, interpretation, or outcome prediction.
2. Never say the animal is safe to wait or does not need a veterinarian.
3. Identify the operator as a veterinary assistant, not a veterinarian.
4. Include the standard educational-scope and emergency reminder.
5. When uncertain, decline and refer to a licensed veterinarian.
